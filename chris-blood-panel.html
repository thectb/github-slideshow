Clinical Recommendations

Prioritized Interventions
Elevated Cortisol — Stress Management & Recovery Protocol
Priority

Morning cortisol at 19.2 μg/dL exceeds the upper reference limit of 18.0 μg/dL, suggesting chronic HPA axis activation. Given your athletic profile and otherwise optimal biomarkers (including robust testosterone at 821 ng/dL), overtraining or insufficient recovery is the most likely etiology. Chronically elevated cortisol can impair muscle recovery, suppress immune function, disrupt sleep quality, increase injury risk, and eventually lead to HPA axis dysregulation or burnout. Immediate interventions: (1) Reduce total training volume by 20-30% for 4 weeks to assess response. (2) Prioritize sleep hygiene targeting 8+ hours nightly with consistent schedule. (3) Incorporate active recovery days and deload weeks into training periodization. (4) Implement stress management techniques including meditation, breathwork, or mindfulness practice 10-15 minutes daily. (5) Consider phosphatidylserine supplementation (400-600 mg/day) to modulate cortisol response. (6) Evaluate and optimize nutrition periodization, ensuring adequate caloric intake and carbohydrate timing around training. Recheck morning cortisol and consider 4-point salivary cortisol curve in 4-6 weeks to assess rhythm and response to intervention. If elevation persists despite recovery protocols, consider endocrinology consultation to rule out other etiologies.
